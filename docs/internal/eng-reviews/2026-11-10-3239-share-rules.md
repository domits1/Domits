Covers `hostowners/utils/shareRules.js` with its tests and the `shareRules.cases.json` fixture, and the `hostOwners.shares` texts in the four language files. The rules are pure functions for the set-shares form that comes in the next pull request. They reuse `basisPoints.js`, `utcDate.js` and `ARCHIVED_OWNER_STATUS` from `ownerFormRules.js`; none of those files change.

What the rules do, and the decisions behind them:

- The current split of a property is built by grouping the owners list (`owner.properties[]`) by `property_id`. There is no read route for one property, and set-shares only accepts owners of the caller's host, so the owners list holds the whole current split.
- Archived owners are not selectable, because set-shares answers `409` for them. Inactive and pending owners are.
- An empty owner list is refused in V1. The backend accepts it and ends all ownership of the property, which is too easy to do by accident from a form.
- No automatic rounding. A percentage becomes basis points through `percentToBasisPoints`, with at most 2 decimals, and the total must be exactly 10000. Three equal owners are 33.33, 33.33 and 33.34.
- A 0% share is refused, because the backend minimum is 1 basis point. The host removes the owner instead.
- Each owner appears once, compared without letter case.
- The date comes from a native date input and is checked in UTC, from today up to 1826 days ahead, the same as the backend.
- A comma is accepted as the decimal sign, so `33,33` is 33.33. Only a value with exactly one comma is changed; `basisPoints.js` stays as strict as the backend.
- A planned change is detected when a current period of the property has an end date.
- `validateShares` returns translation keys only. A test checks that every key it can return has a text in all four languages.

Tests: 77 share rule tests in `shareRules.test.js`, 35 of them from the fixture (26 percent cases, 9 date cases). They cover grouping, owners without shares, archived owners, 33.33/33.33/33.34, more than 2 decimals, 0%, negative and non-numeric values, comma values (valid and invalid), a duplicate owner, totals of 99.99 and 100.01, the empty list, and dates of yesterday, today, today plus 1826 and today plus 1827 days with a fixed clock. They also cover UTC near midnight, the exact payload shape and planned changes. All 425 tests in `hostowners` pass.

Mutation checks. Each change below was made on purpose, and at least one test failed for each: 0% accepted, an empty list accepted, archived owners selectable, the comma ignored, and the payload built without the comma. A rule that changes every comma instead of exactly one also passed the tests. It is equivalent: after the first comma becomes a dot, a second comma still fails the strict pattern, so the result is the same.
